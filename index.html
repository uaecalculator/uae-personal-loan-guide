UAE Personal Loan Guide: Debt Burden Ratio, Loan Caps and Monthly Payments

Plain-language explanations of how personal loans are sized and priced in the United Arab Emirates, with the Central Bank rules behind each figure and worked examples you can check by hand.

Most loan questions in the UAE come down to three numbers. How much can I borrow on my salary? What will the monthly payment be? And why did the bank approve less than I asked for, or refuse the application outright? Behind almost every answer sits one ratio that lenders check before anything else.

That ratio is the debt burden ratio, and this guide explains how it works, which Central Bank caps sit around it, and how a monthly payment is built. The companion Loan & Savings guides on the main site carry the working tools for each figure. Read the section that matches your question here, then run your own numbers there.

Good to know: every figure on this page is an estimate built from the standard Central Bank rules. Your bank's own policy, your credit record and the exact terms of an offer decide the final amount you can borrow.

What Is the Debt Burden Ratio?

The debt burden ratio, usually shortened to DBR, compares everything you pay towards debt each month with the gross income you earn each month. Add up the monthly payments, divide by the monthly salary, and the result is a percentage. Central Bank rules cap that percentage at 50 percent for all borrowers, so no lender is supposed to let your total debt payments pass half of your gross income.

What counts as a monthly obligation?

  • The instalment on any existing personal loan.
  • The monthly payment on car finance or any other loan.
  • Credit cards, assessed at 5 percent of the card limit per month. This 5 percent convention is bank practice, applied by lenders when they test an application.
  • The payment on the new loan you are applying for, tested before approval.

Rent, school fees and utility bills do not enter the ratio, because they are living costs rather than debt payments. That is why two people on the same salary can receive very different offers: the one with a car payment and two large card limits may already sit near the cap, while the one with no other debts has the full 50 percent available for a new loan.

Why does the ratio matter more than the salary?

A high salary does not by itself produce a large loan. The bank first subtracts the space your existing obligations already occupy, and only the headroom left under 50 percent can hold a new payment. When an application is cut down or declined, the explanation is nearly always in this subtraction. You can check your debt burden ratio with your own figures, and the step-by-step guide to how the DBR is calculated walks through the same method lenders use.

A Worked DBR Example, Step by Step

Take a borrower with a gross monthly salary of AED 15,000, an existing car payment and one credit card. The card carries no balance, but it still counts, because the assessment uses the limit rather than the balance.

ItemMonthly amount
Gross monthly salaryAED 15,000
Car finance paymentAED 1,800
Credit card: limit AED 30,000 assessed at 5 percentAED 1,500
Total monthly obligationsAED 3,300
Debt burden ratio (3,300 divided by 15,000)22 percent
Maximum allowed at the 50 percent capAED 7,500
Headroom left for a new loan paymentAED 4,200

The steps are worth following slowly. The card adds AED 1,500 because 5 percent of AED 30,000 is AED 1,500, even though the borrower owes nothing on it. Total obligations of AED 3,300 against a salary of AED 15,000 give a ratio of 22 percent. The cap allows payments up to AED 7,500 a month, so AED 4,200 of monthly headroom remains.

Headroom converts into a loan size once a rate and term are chosen. At a reducing rate of 5 percent over 48 months, a monthly payment of AED 4,200 supports a loan of about AED 182,000. The same borrower with no car payment and no card would have AED 7,500 of headroom, and the binding limit would then be the salary multiple described in the next section rather than the ratio.

Tip: run the ratio with your real card limits, not your card balances. The gap between the two is the single most common reason a borrower's own estimate is higher than the bank's.

What Are the Central Bank Caps on Personal Loans?

Four Central Bank rules frame every personal loan offer in the UAE. They apply across lenders, and a bank can be stricter than the rules but not looser.

RuleLimit
Debt burden ratioTotal debt payments capped at 50 percent of gross monthly income
Loan sizeMaximum 20 times the monthly salary
TenureMaximum 48 months to repay
Early settlement feeMaximum 1 percent of the outstanding amount, plus 5 percent VAT

Which cap binds first?

It depends on the salary and the debts already in place. Consider a borrower earning AED 10,000 a month with no existing obligations. The size cap allows a loan of AED 200,000, which is 20 times the salary. At 5 percent reducing over 48 months, that loan carries a payment of AED 4,605.86, about 46 percent of the salary, so it fits inside the ratio cap with room to spare. Working from the ratio alone, a payment of AED 5,000 would support a loan of about AED 217,000, which the size cap then cuts back to AED 200,000. For a debt-free borrower on this salary, the multiple binds first.

Reverse the picture and the ratio binds first, as the worked example above showed: existing payments of AED 3,300 on a salary of AED 15,000 left headroom for a loan near AED 182,000, well under the AED 300,000 size ceiling for that salary. This is why asking a bank "how much can I borrow" never has one answer: the binding cap changes with your obligations.

What happened to the minimum salary rule?

For years, Central Bank rules set a minimum salary of AED 5,000 for personal loan eligibility. That requirement was removed in November 2025. There is no longer a single regulatory floor, and each bank now applies its own income policy, so a salary one lender accepts may fall below another lender's internal minimum. The removal widened access on paper, but the DBR test still decides what any given salary can carry.

Pensioners face a tighter test: repayments on loans held by retired borrowers are capped at 30 percent of the pension income rather than 50 percent, reflecting the Central Bank's more cautious stance for borrowers without employment income.

How Is a Monthly Loan Payment Worked Out?

UAE personal loans are priced on a reducing balance. Each month, interest is charged on the balance still outstanding, the payment is split between that interest and a slice of the principal, and the balance falls. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal. The payment itself stays the same throughout the term, which is what makes the figure easy to budget around.

The anchor example: AED 100,000 over 48 months

Borrow AED 100,000 at a reducing rate of 5 percent a year, repaid over 48 months. The monthly payment is AED 2,302.93. Across the full term you repay AED 110,540.64, so the total interest is AED 10,540.64. Every personal loan quote in the UAE can be tested against this shape: same amount, same term, and the only honest difference between offers is the rate and the fees. How does the term change the payment?

A shorter term raises the monthly payment and cuts the total interest sharply. The same AED 100,000 at 5 percent reducing costs AED 8,560.75 a month over 12 months with AED 2,729.00 of interest, AED 4,387.14 a month over 24 months with AED 5,291.36 of interest, and AED 2,997.09 a month over 36 months with AED 7,895.24 of interest. Stretching to the 48 month maximum drops the payment to AED 2,302.93 but lifts the interest to AED 10,540.64. Borrowers who choose the longest term for comfort pay for that comfort in interest, and the trade is worth pricing before signing.

Flat Rate or Reducing Rate: Which One Is Real?

Some offers are quoted with a flat rate, and the flat number always looks smaller. The two rates measure different things. A flat rate charges interest on the original loan amount for the entire term, ignoring the fact that the balance falls with every payment. A reducing rate charges interest only on the balance still owed, which is how UAE personal loans are actually priced.

The same loan, both ways

Take AED 100,000 over 48 months again. At a flat rate of 5 percent, interest is 5 percent of AED 100,000 for each of the four years, which is AED 20,000 in total, and the monthly payment is AED 2,500. At a reducing rate of 5 percent, the interest is AED 10,540.64 and the payment is AED 2,302.93. The flat quote costs almost twice as much interest at the same headline number. Converted properly, a flat rate of 5 percent over 48 months equals a reducing rate of about 9.24 percent. Central Bank rules require lenders to show the reducing equivalent alongside any flat quote, so compare offers on the reducing figure and on the total repayment, never on the flat headline.

Watch for: a quote that leads with a low flat rate and mentions the reducing equivalent only in the small print. The small print figure is the one that decides what you pay.

How Do Credit Cards Affect a Loan Application?

Credit cards are the quiet term in the DBR test. Lenders assess each card at 5 percent of its limit per month, as bank practice, whether the card is maxed out or has never been used. A wallet with three cards carrying combined limits of AED 90,000 adds AED 4,500 a month to assessed obligations, which on a salary of AED 15,000 consumes 30 of the 50 percentage points available before any loan payment is counted.

What can you do about card limits?

  • Close cards you genuinely do not use, and get the closure confirmed before you apply.
  • Ask the issuing bank to reduce the limit on cards you keep but rarely stretch.
  • Pay down revolving balances, which helps your credit record even though the limit is what the ratio counts.
  • Apply for the loan after the changes show on your credit file, not in the same week.

None of this changes your salary or the loan you want. It changes only the assessed obligations standing in front of the new payment, which is why borrowers are often surprised at how much an offer improves after a single unused card is closed.

What Does Early Settlement Cost?

Settling a loan early means paying the outstanding balance in one go before the term ends. Central Bank rules cap the early settlement fee at 1 percent of the outstanding amount, plus 5 percent VAT on the fee. On an outstanding balance of AED 60,000, the fee is AED 600, the VAT is AED 30, and the total cost of closing the loan is AED 630 on top of the balance itself.

Whether settlement is worth it depends on the interest still to come. Interest on a reducing loan is front-loaded, so a loan in its final year carries little remaining interest, and paying a fee to escape it may save almost nothing. A loan in its first half, at a high rate, usually saves far more in future interest than the fee costs. Ask the bank for the settlement figure in writing, run the remaining interest on the current schedule, and compare the two numbers before deciding.

How to Prepare Before You Apply

Five habits line an application up with the rules the bank will apply, so the offer you get is the offer the caps allow.

  1. Add up your real obligations. List every loan payment and car instalment, then add 5 percent of each card limit. Divide by your gross monthly salary to see your current ratio.
  2. Work backwards from the headroom. Subtract your obligations from 50 percent of your salary. The remainder is the largest new payment you can carry, and it sets the loan size at any given rate and term.
  3. Compare offers on the reducing rate and the total repayment. A flat headline hides the true cost. Two offers with the same total repayment are genuinely equal, whatever their headlines say.
  4. Price more than one term. The 48 month maximum payment is the gentlest per month and the most expensive overall. A 36 month schedule on the same loan can save thousands in interest.
  5. Trim card limits first. Closing an unused card or cutting a limit is the fastest way to open headroom, and it costs nothing.

After working through the figures, hold them against the documents the bank will see: your salary certificate, your bank statements and your credit report. If your own ratio and the bank's are likely to differ, the difference almost always traces back to a card limit or an obligation you forgot to count.

Why Does One Ratio Decide So Much?

The DBR exists to keep total borrowing survivable. A borrower whose payments take 50 percent of gross income still has the other half for rent, family costs and the unexpected, and the cap is set at the point where lenders judge that balance to hold across the whole market. The 20 times salary rule and the 48 month limit work as outer fences: they stop a debt-free borrower from taking a loan so large that even a ratio-compliant payment would rest on an unrealistic view of the future.

Understanding the ratio changes how you shop for a loan. Instead of asking each bank for the largest amount it will give, you arrive knowing your headroom, the payment it supports and the total interest at each term. The negotiation becomes about rate and fees, which is the ground where banks actually compete.

Short Answers

What is the maximum DBR allowed in the UAE?

50 percent of gross monthly income, across all of your debt payments combined.

What is the largest personal loan on a salary of AED 15,000?

The size cap allows AED 300,000, which is 20 times the salary, provided your other obligations leave enough headroom under the DBR cap.

What is the longest repayment term for a personal loan?

48 months. Personal loans in the UAE cannot run longer than four years.

How is a credit card counted in the DBR?

At 5 percent of the card limit per month, as bank practice, regardless of the balance on the card.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the debt burden ratio in the UAE?

The debt burden ratio, or DBR, is your total monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income, shown as a percentage. Central Bank rules cap it at 50 percent for all borrowers, so your combined loan payments, car finance instalments and the assessed cost of your credit cards cannot take more than half of your monthly salary.

What counts towards the DBR?

Every regular debt payment counts: personal loan instalments, car finance, and any other loan payments. Credit cards are assessed differently. Banks count 5 percent of the card limit each month, whether or not you carry a balance, and that 5 percent convention is bank practice rather than a line in the Central Bank rulebook. Rent and utility bills are not debt payments and do not enter the ratio.

What is the maximum personal loan I can get in the UAE?

Two caps apply together. A personal loan cannot exceed 20 times your monthly salary, and it must be repaid within 48 months. On a salary of AED 15,000 the size ceiling is AED 300,000. Your existing debts may lower the amount a bank can offer, because the new payment also has to fit inside the 50 percent DBR cap.

How much is the monthly payment on a loan of AED 100,000?

At a reducing rate of 5 percent a year over 48 months, the monthly payment on AED 100,000 is AED 2,302.93. Over the full term you repay AED 110,540.64, so the total interest is AED 10,540.64. A higher rate or a longer term changes the payment, and a longer term always raises the total interest.

Is a flat rate cheaper than a reducing rate?

No. A flat rate charges interest on the original loan amount for the whole term, even though the balance falls every month. A reducing rate charges interest only on what you still owe. Over 48 months, a flat rate of 5 percent works out at about 9.24 percent reducing, so the flat quote that looks lower is usually the more expensive loan. Banks must show the reducing equivalent next to any flat quote.

Do credit cards I barely use still affect a loan application?

Yes. Banks assess each card at 5 percent of its limit per month, so a card with a limit of AED 30,000 adds AED 1,500 to your monthly obligations even if the balance is zero. Closing a card you do not need, or asking the bank to lower its limit, reduces that assessed amount before you apply.

Is there still a minimum salary for a personal loan in the UAE?

The Central Bank minimum salary requirement of AED 5,000 was removed in November 2025. There is no longer a single regulatory floor. Each bank now applies its own salary policy, so the minimum income for approval differs from one lender to the next.

What does it cost to settle a personal loan early?

The early settlement fee is capped at 1 percent of the outstanding amount, plus 5 percent VAT on the fee. On an outstanding balance of AED 60,000 the fee is AED 600 and the VAT is AED 30, so settling early costs AED 630 on top of the balance itself.

Can I have personal loans with two different banks?

The caps apply to your total obligations, not to each bank separately. Combined payments across every lender still have to stay within 50 percent of your gross income, and lenders check your overall position through the credit bureau before approving a new loan. A second application is judged on the headroom left after the first loan, not on a fresh allowance.

Why did the bank offer me less than the amount I asked for?

The usual reason is headroom. The bank adds your existing payments and 5 percent of each card limit to the payment on the loan you requested, and if the total passes 50 percent of your salary, it cuts the loan size until the ratio fits. A large unused card limit is the most common hidden cause, because it consumes headroom even with a zero balance.
